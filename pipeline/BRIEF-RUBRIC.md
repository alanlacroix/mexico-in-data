# The Brief

The homepage edition contains one to three of the most consequential developments
for a reader trying to understand Mexico. Code first narrows the day's reporting to
24 trustworthy candidates. One bounded ranking call may return at most five; code
locks only the top three as the edition pool.

Prefer concrete changes in government policy, regulation, courts, security, trade,
macroeconomic data, or company investment. Exclude opinion, advice, profiles,
previews, routine market moves, announcements without a concrete consequence, and
duplicate angles. A scheduled official outcome always enters the candidate set and
outranks ordinary news, including an unchanged rate decision.

Every published card is one complete bilingual unit:

- Headline: the shortest accurate account of what happened.
- Context: one additional sourced fact or comparison.
- Background: the structural fact a newcomer needs, citing evidence beyond the
  original article.
- Our view: a narrow inference supported by its cited evidence.
- What we're watching: the next observable decision, release, or result.

The factual line uses ordinary actor-action-fact language. Briefly Explained never
uses first person or narrates prompts, labels, or source fields. Every field cites its
exact retained evidence. The drafting call writes both languages. A deterministic
gate checks them, then one independent closed-evidence pass compares English and
Spanish against the same field-level citations and either approves or rejects the
whole card. There is no copy retry and no model web search.

On weekdays, at least one published story must be from the exact editorial date.
Stories from the previous date may appear only as visibly dated Key developments.
On weekends, the recap may draw from Monday through the current day and includes any
new weekend development. The topic-filtered weekly shelf contains only cards from
editions published during the current week.

The entire English/Spanish candidate is validated in memory. Zero passing stories,
a missing exact-day story, a rejected required scheduled outcome, incomplete Spanish,
bad evidence, a timeout, or a budget failure makes the command fail and leaves the
last public edition unchanged.
