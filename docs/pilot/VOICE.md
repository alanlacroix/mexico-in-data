# MexicoBrief voice

Write for an informed reader with limited time. Lead with what happened. Follow with the evidence and the explanation the reader needs to understand it.

References reviewed:
- https://ramp.com/data/ai-companies-influencer-marketing
- https://assets.stripeassets.com/fzn2n1nzq965/3LlGw839Q6kUwxZlLZDtH6/75ddcbada4aa7743dd8ec7d0f9ca497e/Stripe-annual-letter-2025-desktop.pdf

Borrow their specificity, concrete comparisons and explanatory sequence. Do not borrow product promotion or imply that MexicoBrief owns their kind of first-party data. Attribute reporting and distinguish our interpretation.

Before approving each edition:
- Replace vague claims with named actors, dates, measures and actions already supported by sources.
- Cut sentences announcing what the reader will learn or saying that a distinction matters. Explain the distinction itself.
- Keep ranking deliberations in the editorial record; don't repeatedly sell each story's importance in the body.
- Avoid slogans, fabricated urgency, generic business advice and claims that the reader now knows everything necessary.
- Make qualifications specific. Do not attach a general disclaimer to every fact.
- Check the headline, thread, paragraph and margin for duplicate information. A margin must add something or be omitted in a future template revision; never fill space with a slogan.
- Preserve natural sentence variety and sound Spanish. Shorter is useful only when meaning survives.
- Never add a statistic, causal explanation or forecast merely to make the prose more interesting.
